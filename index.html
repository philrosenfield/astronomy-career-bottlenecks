Default: 3.7% per year, informed by doctoral enrollment in astronomy/astrophysics units growing from 1,236 in 2017 to 1,648 in 2025 (GSS Table 1-3). Growth in 2024-25 was only 0.6%. Applying an enrollment trend to future funded starts is an assumption, not a forecast or a measured admissions rate.

    Shared assumptions and limits

    The following are illustrative assumptions, applied to both paths. Preferences and hiring openings have not been estimated from the opening headcounts.

    • This is a deterministic, annual dynamical system. Each year's training cohorts, searches, and occupied jobs feed the following year's transitions. It follows the opening students and postdocs plus later PhD applicants, not the whole U.S. astronomy workforce.
    • Annual PhD applicants are unique people seeking a place, not application forms or all bachelor's graduates. New admissions are limited by the planned funded annual intake. Nonadmitted applicants remain counted, but their later careers and repeat applications are not modeled.
    • Annual funded starts before staff transfer equal the starting intake times (1 + growth percent / 100) raised to (year - 1). Growth uses the same calendar in both paths; it does not restart when the intervention begins. Applicants, postdoc places, and job openings stay at their separate assumptions. More funding alone cannot create applicants.
    • Annual intake changes new cohorts only. Existing students finish on their original schedule. With a fixed doctoral duration, a change to starts affects completions that many years later. This is an illustrative delay, not a measured prediction. Current funded intake times doctoral duration describes enrollment only if that intake remained constant and enough applicants were available; it is not a forecast under growth.
    • For this demonstration, related work outside academia means astronomy/space-related work, such as government research or space-focused industry. A general technology job is not automatically classified as astronomy-related. All opening counts for those job categories are assumptions.
    • Opening students and postdocs are spread evenly across remaining training years. Everyone in the doctoral stage completes after the assumed fixed duration. This omits noncompletion and does not infer a graduation rate from a completer median.
    • Academic applicants include interested postdocs and doctorate completers. Continuing openings are filled proportionally among eligible applicants. Postdocs already employed can finish their remaining modeled time even when new capacity is lower.
    • Someone who wants academia first seeks a continuing role or a postdoc. After the postdoc limit or the assumed waiting window, an unsuccessful applicant stops the academic search and seeks astronomy-related work outside academia.
    • Someone who voluntarily leaves the academic route chooses related work or another career according to the shared preference assumption. Related-job seekers try for the assumed waiting window, then broaden their search to other work. Unmatched applicants remain visible in the search pool.
    • The matching probability is the fraction of a candidate pool expected to match each year, capped by the number of openings. Career preparation can change this assumed probability but cannot create openings. An unsuccessful search can reflect limited positions or matching friction.
    • Each year's planned starts before staff transfer times doctoral duration define that year's reserved recurring training-support budget. Each requested staff position reduces funded annual starts by its relative support cost divided by doctoral duration. Staff hiring waits until occupied doctoral places and staff support fit that budget. Growing budgets or vacant funded places can release support too, so funding growth can bring staff hiring forward. Lower budgets can temporarily be exceeded while existing students finish; they are not dismissed. Funders are assumed to permit transfers. Support costs do not measure scientific output.
    • Existing faculty, permanent staff, and earlier leavers are not reconstructed. Later international recruitment directly into postdocs, emigration, and other-field career changes are omitted. There is no predoc program, national workforce calibration, dollar budget, retirement model, or forecast of future funding.

    Measured opening counts: GSS 2024, Table 2-4, row 60, columns G and L. Its academic-unit field definition differs from AIP department rosters and SED doctorate fields. Those series are not combined into a single inferred population.

    This demonstration explains mechanisms under declared assumptions. It does not estimate how many real astronomers were forced out. Deterministic fractional cohorts can produce fractional people.
